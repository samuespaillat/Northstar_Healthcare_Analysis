# Northstar Healthcare Network: Business Operations & Financial Analysis

## Project Overview

Northstar Healthcare Network is a fictional healthcare organization operating multiple outpatient clinics. Management has raised concerns about patient wait times, appointment performance, staffing capacity, and operating expenses. This dataset is entirely fictional and was generated through the use of OpenAI ChatGPT. 

The goal of this project was to analyze the organization's operational and financial data, identify areas of concern, and provide recommendations on where management should focus its attention.

Using Excel, SQL, and Power BI, I explored appointment trends, patient access, staffing coverage, and financial activity across clinics and departments.

## Business Objectives

The analysis focused on six key questions:

1. Which clinics and departments have the longest patient wait times?
2. Where are cancellation and no-show rates highest?
3. How effectively is available staffing capacity being covered?
4. How do charges, estimated reimbursements, direct costs, and operating expenses vary across the organization?
5. What changes can be observed in appointments, charges, and expenses over time?
6. Which clinics and departments should management prioritize for further investigation?

## Tools Used

- Excel
- MySQL
- Power BI

## Key Findings

### Patient Access
- Department 2 had the highest average wait time at approximately 31.19 days.
- Department 5 had the lowest average wait time at approximately 12.90 days.
- Average wait times were relatively similar across appointment types.
- Departments 2 and 3 were identified as areas for further investigation.

### Appointment Performance
- Department 1 recorded the highest appointment volume, with 2,756 appointments.
- Department 3 had an uncompleted appointment rate of approximately 21.96%.
- Clinic 1 had the highest uncompleted appointment rate among clinics, at approximately 21.33%.

### Staffing
- Overall staffing coverage was approximately 85%.
- Staffing coverage varied across clinic and department combinations.
- The analysis did not establish that additional hiring was necessary. Existing staffing allocation and scheduling practices should be reviewed first.

### Financial Performance
- Total charges were approximately $1.76 million.
- Total operating expenses were approximately $17.21 million.
- Estimated reimbursements were approximately $915,150.
- Estimated direct costs were approximately $633,420.

Monthly analysis also revealed periods when charges and appointment volumes declined while operating expenses increased. These movements raised questions about expense timing, budget allocation, and cost management.

## Management Recommendations

Based on the findings, I identified two primary areas for management to investigate.

### 1. Business Administration and Expense Management

Management should review how expenses are distributed and monitored, particularly during periods when appointment volumes and charges decline but expenses increase.

Areas worth investigating include budget allocation, labor costs, supplier pricing, equipment expenses, and the timing of monthly expenditures.

### 2. Patient Access and Scheduling

Long wait times in Departments 2 and 3 warrant further investigation. Before considering additional hiring, management should evaluate existing staffing resources, scheduling practices, and opportunities to improve operational efficiency.

Potential areas of improvement include staff training, scheduling tools, integrated check-in systems, and offering patients appointments at other clinics when earlier availability exists.

### Additional Areas for Investigation

Clinics 5, 6, and 7 had comparatively lower patient volumes and displayed different appointment and financial trends. Further investigation could help determine whether these differences are related to local demand, clinic accessibility, operating costs, or patient preferences.

Department 1 also warrants further review because of the observed differences between appointment volume, charges, and expenses.

These recommendations are intended as areas for further investigation rather than immediate operational changes.

## Limitations

The dataset covers approximately one year of activity, limiting the ability to identify reliable seasonal or long-term trends.

Other limitations include:

- Charges represent billed amounts, not confirmed collected revenue.
- Reimbursements are estimates rather than verified payments.
- Estimated direct costs and operating expenses are separate measures.
- The available information does not support a definitive profitability calculation.
- Staffing coverage does not represent actual capacity utilization.
- The data cannot establish why patients cancel appointments or whether they seek care elsewhere.
- Observed changes in metrics do not establish causation.

All data used in this project is synthetic and does not represent a real healthcare organization or actual patients.

## Conclusion

This project provided an opportunity to examine how operational and financial data can help identify business concerns and support management decisions.

The analysis highlighted potential issues involving patient access, appointment performance, staffing coverage, and expense management. Rather than making assumptions based on limited information, the recommendations focus on validating the findings and investigating specific clinics and departments before considering broader changes.

The overall objective was to move beyond reporting numbers and use the available data to identify meaningful business questions and potential opportunities for improvement.
